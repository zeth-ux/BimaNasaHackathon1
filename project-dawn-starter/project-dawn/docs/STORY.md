# PROJECT DAWN — Storyline & Objectives

NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026 · Space Mission Design Game
**Worlds:** Earth · Moon · Mars

> **Theme:** Every technology built for the rocket becomes a gift to the people in the bunkers.
> (This is how NASA "spinoffs" work in real life: space technology improving everyday life on Earth.)

---

## 1. The Premise

A gamma-ray burst from a distant dying star damages Earth's ozone layer, and deadly UV light floods the surface. *(The idea comes from a real hypothesis: some scientists think a burst like this damaged the ozone layer about 440 million years ago and contributed to the Ordovician mass extinction.)*

Humanity survives underground in scattered **bunkers**, but they are cut off from each other. Nobody can stay outside long enough to repair the radio towers, and the old satellites are failing one by one with no one to replace them.

In **Bunker 4**, four of the world's greatest scientists make a decision: humanity will not wait in the dark. The ozone layer will rebuild itself from Earth's own oxygen, but slowly. Their plan is **Project Dawn**:

1. Build a **shield in space** that protects Earth while the ozone heals
2. Help every bunker **survive and reconnect** until the day they can walk outside again

---

## 2. Characters

| Scientist | Profession | Game role | What they decide |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Engineer** | Aerospace engineer | Engineer | Builds rockets, platforms and satellites; manages mass |
| **The Scientist** | Atmospheric and planetary scientist | Scientist | Studies the sky, chooses instruments, leads research |
| **The Navigator** | Orbital mechanics expert | Navigator | Plans trajectories, fuel and launch windows |
| **The Quartermaster** | Systems and logistics engineer | Quartermaster | Runs resources, power and the bunker network |

**CERES** is the bunker's mission-control AI and the players' tutor. It is named after the dwarf planet visited by NASA's real Dawn spacecraft, which the project honors. CERES advises and teaches, but only the four scientists decide.

## 3. Main Objectives (5 Acts × 2 objectives = 10)

### ACT 1 — "First Light" · Earth
**Big objective: Learn how bad the sky really is.**

| # | Objective | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 1.1 | **Build the launch site** | Clear a launch pad near the bunker and set up a ground station to talk to spacecraft |
| 1.2 | **Launch the first CubeSat** | Build a tiny satellite from salvaged parts (a smartphone brain, like NASA's PhoneSat) and map the ozone damage. This starts the **ozone meter**. |

**Disaster:** UV storm
**Story turn:** the damage is global. Launching heavy satellites against Earth's strong gravity costs too much fuel.
> CERES: *"Then don't build it here. Build it where leaving is easy."*

---

### ACT 2 — "Water Above Us" · Moon
**Big objective: Find fuel beyond Earth.**

| # | Objective | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 2.1 | **Scout the Moon** | Flyby → orbiter → lander: find water ice in shadowed craters near the south pole |
| 2.2 | **Build the lunar outpost** | Land the outpost on a sunlit crater rim, mine the ice and split it into rocket fuel (hydrogen + oxygen) |

**Disaster:** a solar particle storm. With no air or magnetic field, the crew learns why the radiation shelter isn't optional.

---

### ACT 3 — "The Moon Factory" · Moon
**Big objective: Build the shield.**

| # | Objective | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 3.1 | **Build the factory** | Turn lunar soil into metal and glass for satellite parts |
| 3.2 | **Launch the first shield satellites** | The Moon's gravity is one-sixth of Earth's, so each launch is cheap. The shield starts protecting Earth. |

**Disaster:** an asteroid on course for Earth (see optional objective G)
**Story turn:** the shield works, but the bunkers are running out of food.
> CERES: *"Earth's sky now looks like Mars's sky. Mars has lived with that for billions of years. Let's learn from it."*

---

### ACT 4 — "The Red Teacher" · Mars
**Big objective: Learn to live under a broken sky.**

| # | Objective | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 4.1 | **Scout Mars** | Flyby → orbiter → lander: survive entry with a heat shield, and 12-hour nights with batteries. Study how Mars lost most of its atmosphere (NASA's MAVEN mission). |
| 4.2 | **Build the UV-safe greenhouse** | Grow crops under UV-filtering domes, using oxygen made from Martian air (like NASA's MOXIE experiment) |

**Disaster:** a global dust storm blacks out solar power, as one did to NASA's Opportunity rover in 2018.

---

### ACT 5 — "Dawn" · Mars → Earth
**Big objective: Bring humanity back to the surface.**

| # | Objective | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 5.1 | **Launch home from Mars** | The first rocket ever to lift off from Mars, fueled on site, carries seeds and greenhouse samples to Earth |
| 5.2 | **Complete the shield** | The last shield satellites take position. The ozone meter climbs as the sky heals, and the bunker doors open. |

**Final scene:** the four scientists step outside without suits and watch a real sunrise: a **dawn**.

---

## 4. Optional Objectives — "Helping Humanity"

Each optional objective unlocks from technology the crew already built for a main mission. Completing it helps the bunkers and gives a gameplay reward.

| # | Optional objective | Unlocked by | How it helps humanity | Game reward |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | **Bunker Link:** communication relay between all bunkers | The rocket's radio system (1.2) | Bunkers can talk, share supplies and coordinate for the first time since the disaster | More workers and resources join the project |
| **B** | **Platform Shield:** UV canopy over the launch platform | Launch site build (1.1) | Workers can safely build outside the bunker | Faster construction on Earth |
| **C** | **Water for All:** ice-cleaning tech shared with bunkers | Lunar ice processing (2.2) | Clean drinking water for bunkers whose supplies are failing | Population health and morale go up |
| **D** | **Storm Watch:** space-weather satellite | Solar storm survival (2.2) | Early warning before solar storms damage bunker power systems | Disaster warnings on all worlds |
| **E** | **Harvest Eye:** weather and farming satellite | First shield satellite design (3.2) | Weather forecasts and crop monitoring for the first surface farms | Bonus food and credits |
| **F** | **Disaster Watch:** natural disaster detection satellite | Shield satellite design (3.2) | Warns bunkers of floods, wildfires and earthquakes; guides rescue teams | Fewer losses from disasters |
| **G** | **Planetary Defense:** deflect the incoming asteroid (DART-style) | Moon factory (3.1) | Protects Earth and the bunkers from an impact | Prevents a major disaster; bonus science |
| **H** | **Greenhouse for Every Bunker:** send the Mars greenhouse design home | Mars greenhouse (4.2) | Bunkers grow their own fresh food | Survival bonus; unlocks a better ending |
| **I** | **Pathfinder:** navigation satellites (like GPS) | Navigator's orbit work (5.1) | Guides people safely between bunkers and to new settlements | Reveals the map |
| **J** | **The Mars Mystery:** investigate a strange methane signal | Mars instruments (4.1) | Works on one of humanity's oldest questions: are we alone? (NASA's Curiosity has measured real, still-unexplained methane spikes on Mars.) | Bonus science; special ending line |

**Mars Mystery rules:** no aliens appear. With the right instruments and sterilized landers the crew gathers better clues, and the honest result is *"Inconclusive: here is what a future mission would need."* If the crew skipped sterilization, the exciting reading turns out to be their own Earth bacteria.

---

## 5. Endings

The main ending is always reached, but **how humanity comes back** depends on the optional objectives:

| Optional objectives completed | Ending | What the player sees |
|---|---|---|
| 0–3 | **Quiet Dawn** | The sky heals, but bunkers open slowly, alone and hungry |
| 4–7 | **Rising Dawn** | Connected bunkers, early farms, a careful return to the surface |
| 8–10 | **Golden Dawn** | A reconnected world with forecasts, farms, safe skies and a new future in space |

**Lesson for players:** space missions aren't only about rockets. They're about what they give back to people on Earth.

```
                                           ↓
                      Ending depends on how many people you helped
```

## 7. Real NASA Connections

| Story element | Real NASA basis |
|---|---|
| Ozone damage from a gamma-ray burst | Ordovician extinction hypothesis |
| Ozone heals itself once the cause stops | Montreal Protocol: ozone projected to return to 1980 levels around 2040 (Antarctic around 2066) |
| Smartphone CubeSat | NASA PhoneSat; CubeSat Launch Initiative |
| Lunar ice for fuel | Water ice in permanently shadowed craters near the lunar south pole |
| Asteroid deflection | NASA DART (2022) |
| Mars atmosphere loss | NASA MAVEN |
| Oxygen from Martian air | MOXIE on Perseverance |
| Dust storm danger | Opportunity rover, ended by a 2018 dust storm |
| Mars methane mystery | Methane spikes measured by Curiosity |
| Weather, farming, disaster and navigation satellites | Real Earth-observing satellites (e.g. Landsat, SMAP), weather satellites, GPS |

---

## 8. Honesty Note (for judges)

The **space UV shield is game fiction**. It is inspired by real proposals for space sunshades, but no such network exists. Everything else in the story is grounded in real science and real NASA missions, listed above.
